01
Serologic Status
Clinical Context
Diagnosis requires at least 1 discrete clinical attack of CNS symptoms. Asymptomatic seropositivity alone — even with compatible MRI — is not sufficient for NMOSD diagnosis. AQP4-IgG stratification determines downstream criteria stringency.
Step 1 · Serologic Testing
What is the patient's AQP4-IgG (aquaporin-4 IgG) serostatus?
▸ Cell-based assay (CBA) is strongly recommended — sensitivity ~76.7%, false-positive rate ~0.1%
▸ IIF and ELISA have lower sensitivity (63–64%) and higher false-positive rates (0.5–1.3%)
▸ Low-titer positive ELISA results require confirmatory testing with CBA
▸ Consider retesting seronegative patients who relapse, or before B-cell / antibody-targeted therapy
✓ Positive
AQP4-IgG detected using best available assay (CBA preferred)
✗ Negative
AQP4-IgG not detected using best available method
? Unavailable
Serologic testing not performed or result unavailable

✓
Symptomatic Cerebral Syndrome
Must be accompanied by NMOSD-typical brain lesions: large confluent white matter, long corpus callosum lesion (≥½ length), corticospinal tract, or extensive periependymal lesions
▾ Additional clinical notes
• No single characteristic is pathognomonic — clinical judgment remains essential
• Altitudinal visual field defects may also result from ischemic optic neuropathy
• Bilateral simultaneous optic neuritis can occur in MS
• Gradually progressive course (vs. attack-based) occurs in only 1–2% of NMOSD — a red flag if present
• "Dissemination in space" for seronegative criteria means ≥2 different core features from ≥2 distinct neuroanatomic regions
• CSF: pleocytosis >50 WBC/μL, neutrophils >5/μL, or eosinophils >5/μL favor NMOSD over MS
03
MRI Requirements
Required for AQP4-IgG Negative / Unknown
For seronegative or untested patients, each applicable core clinical characteristic must be supported by its corresponding MRI finding. Verify MRI requirements for all selected core features below.
▾ MRI characteristics reference
LETM lesion characteristics:
• T2 hyperintense ≥3 contiguous vertebral segments (sagittal)
• Central gray matter predominance (>70% of lesion axially)
• May show T1 hypointensity and gadolinium enhancement
• Cervical lesions may extend rostrally into brainstem
• Cord swelling in acute phase; longitudinally extensive atrophy chronically

Optic nerve MRI:
• Fat-suppressed T2 / STIR with gadolinium T1 enhancement
• Posterior predominance, >½ nerve length, or chiasmal involvement suggest NMOSD

Area postrema / brainstem:
• Dorsal medulla lesions (area postrema): small, often bilateral, or contiguous with cervical cord
• Periependymal fourth ventricular, pontine, and dorsal midbrain lesions

Brain MRI red flags (MS-typical, not NMOSD):
• Dawson's fingers (perpendicular periventricular lesions)
• Inferior temporal lobe periventricular lesions
• Cortical / juxtacortical lesions
• Persistent enhancement >3 months

Red Flags & Alternative Diagnoses
Interpretation Note
No single feature is exclusionary, but red flags warrant thorough investigation for competing diagnoses before confirming NMOSD. The weight of evidence may justify NMOSD diagnosis even with ≥1 red flag present after full workup.

Competing Diagnoses to Exclude
▾ Supportive features that increase NMOSD confidence
CSF supportive of NMOSD (vs. MS):
• Absence of oligoclonal bands (OCB present in <20% NMOSD vs >80% MS)
• Pleocytosis >50 WBC/μL (~35% incidence in NMOSD)
• Neutrophils >5/μL (~44% incidence) or eosinophils >5/μL (~10%)
• Elevated CSF GFAP (peaks days-weeks post-attack)

Systemic autoimmunity (strengthens NMOSD confidence):
• Coexisting SLE or Sjögren syndrome — makes NMOSD (not CNS vasculitis) the more likely cause
• Myasthenia gravis or detectable serum AChR antibodies

Seronegative NMOSD:
• Minority have detectable MOG-IgG (younger, less female-predominant, lower relapse risk)
• Consider retesting: antibody titers increase with relapse, decrease with immunosuppression

Expanded Disability Status Scale (EDSS) — Kurtzke 1983
The EDSS ranges from 0 (normal neurological exam) to 10 (death from MS). It is heavily weighted toward ambulation above EDSS 4.0.

1.0–1.5No disability; minimal signs in one or two functional systems

VZV Immunity Status
Why VZV Matters for MS DMTs
Several high-efficacy DMTs significantly reduce lymphocyte counts or deplete immune cells, reactivating latent VZV (varicella-zoster virus). VZV encephalitis has been reported with fingolimod, siponimod, cladribine, and alemtuzumab.

Key recommendation: VZV IgG should be checked before starting S1P modulators (fingolimod, siponimod, ozanimod), alemtuzumab, and cladribine. If VZV IgG negative (non-immune):
  • Vaccinate with live attenuated varicella vaccine (2 doses) before starting DMT
  • Wait ≥4–6 weeks post-vaccination before initiating lymphocyte-depleting therapy
  • Live vaccines contraindicated during treatment with S1P modulators or alemtuzumab
Gieß et al., Mult Scler 2022; Gilenya prescribing information; Lemtrada REMS

Select all relevant comorbidities. These directly constrain DMT selection — some represent absolute contraindications. Red-highlighted items indicate the most critical safety interactions.
Cardiac
✓
Bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, or significant AV block — contraindication to all S1P modulators (fingolimod, siponimod, ozanimod)
✓
Atrial fibrillation or Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics — first-dose cardiac monitoring required with S1P modulators; cardiology co-management advised
✓
Heart failure (EF <50%) — caution with mitoxantrone (cardiotoxic; cumulative dose limit 140 mg/m²; pre-treatment and annual ECHO required)
Hepatic
✓
Active hepatic disease or cirrhosis (ALT/AST >3× ULN) — caution or contraindication with teriflunomide, dimethyl fumarate, IFN-β, glatiramer; LFTs required for most agents
✓
Hepatitis B (HBV) — mandatory screening before ocrelizumab and other B-cell depleting agents; risk of HBV reactivation with CD20-directed therapies
Ocular
✓
Macular edema or diabetic retinopathy — contraindication to S1P modulators (fingolimod, siponimod); ophthalmologic screening mandatory before initiation
✓
Uveitis or inflammatory eye disease — caution with S1P modulators; discuss with ophthalmology before initiation
Hematologic / Immunologic
✓
Baseline lymphopenia (<1000/µL) — contraindication to dimethyl fumarate initiation; caution with all lymphocyte-depleting agents
✓
Active non-MS autoimmune disease — caution with alemtuzumab (secondary autoimmune AEs occur in ~30–48% of patients over 5 years; thyroid 36%, ITP 2%, nephropathy 0.3%)
✓
HIV or severe immunodeficiency — contraindication to most highly immunosuppressive DMTs; ID and neurology co-management required before any HET
Pulmonary
✓
Severe pulmonary disease (FEV₁ <70% predicted) — fingolimod reduces FEV₁ by ~0.1L and FVC by ~1–2% (FREEDOMS trial); use with caution or avoid
Oncologic History
✓
Active malignancy — alemtuzumab, cladribine, and mitoxantrone carry black-box malignancy warnings; avoid in active cancer
✓
History of breast cancer — breast cancer signal in OPERA I/II trials with ocrelizumab (post-hoc; not causal, but warrants individualized discussion)
CYP2C9 Genotype (for Siponimod only)
Siponimod (Mayzent) is metabolized by CYP2C9. The CYP2C9 *3/*3 genotype results in markedly elevated drug exposure, and siponimod is contraindicated in CYP2C9 *3/*3 poor metabolizers. Genotyping is mandatory before initiation. Approximately 1–2% of European-ancestry patients carry the *3/*3 genotype.
Mayzent prescribing information; Novartis 2019

Step 6 · DMT Recommendations
Ranked by tier based on MS phenotype, disease activity, patient factors, and comorbidities. Review all tiers — a lower-ranked agent may be optimal for an individual patient.
⚖ Shared Decision-Making Note In clinical practice, there are often multiple appropriate DMTs within each tier. The final choice is rarely determined by efficacy data alone. Discussing the adverse effect profiles of each candidate with the patient — and eliciting their values, lifestyle, and risk tolerance — is frequently the most important factor in determining the first-choice agent. This aligns with ECTRIMS/EAN and AAN guidelines emphasizing individualized, patient-centered DMT selection.

Step 1 · Establish Parkinsonism
Prerequisite for applying MDS-PD criteria. Bradykinesia is mandatory; at least one additional cardinal feature required.
A · Bradykinesia MANDATORY
Bradykinesia — slowness + decrement in amplitude or speed with repetition
Slowness of movement AND decrement in amplitude or speed (or progressive hesitations/halts) as movements are continued. Assess via: finger tapping (3.4), hand movements (3.5), pronation-supination (3.6), toe tapping (3.7), foot tapping (3.8). Limb bradykinesia must be documented — voice, face, or axial bradykinesia alone is insufficient for diagnosis.
In PD, a decline in speed or amplitude is seen as movements are continued — a feature sometimes not seen in alternate-etiology parkinsonism. No single UPDRS cutoff defines parkinsonism. Confounding factors (arthritis, weakness) do not automatically prevent diagnosis — clinical judgment determines whether findings are entirely attributable to confounding features.
B · Additional Cardinal Feature ≥1 REQUIRED
Rest Tremor · 4–6 Hz, suppressed by movement initiation
Fully resting limb, suppressed during movement initiation (UPDRS 3.17, 3.18). Kinetic and postural tremors alone do not qualify. Re-emergent tremor after postural hold is acceptable but must also be present at rest. Chin or jaw tremor alone does not count.
Rigidity — lead-pipe, velocity-independent resistance to passive movement
Slow passive movement of major joints with patient relaxed. Distinct from spasticity or paratonia. Isolated cogwheeling without lead-pipe rigidity does not fulfill criteria. Froment maneuver can unmask subtle rigidity.

ⓘ Postural instability is NOT part of the parkinsonism definition — its early presence suggests an alternative diagnosis.

Step 2 · Absolute Exclusion Criteria
Any single exclusion present → PD cannot be diagnosed at any certainty level. Clinical judgment applies — an exclusion from an unrelated cause (e.g., cerebellar hemisphere stroke) may not apply.
1 · Unequivocal cerebellar abnormalities (cerebellar gait, limb ataxia, cerebellar oculomotor signs)
Sustained gaze-evoked nystagmus, macro square-wave jerks, hypermetric saccades. Must be unequivocal. Unilateral signs attributable to a cerebellar hemisphere stroke do not apply.
2 · Downward vertical supranuclear gaze palsy or selectively slowed downward vertical saccades
Pathognomonic for PSP-Richardson syndrome. Confirm supranuclear origin via intact VOR (doll's-eye maneuver). Selective slowing of downward saccades on quantitative testing also qualifies. Isolated upward gaze restriction is non-specific and does not qualify.
3 · Probable bvFTD or primary progressive aphasia (within first 5 years of disease)
Per consensus criteria (Rascovsky et al. Brain 2011). Applies only to frontotemporal-type dementia — other dementias are NOT an exclusion. DLB is explicitly not an "alternative parkinsonian syndrome" per MDS criteria. Do not withhold diagnosis pending the 5-year mark if features are absent.
4 · Parkinsonian features restricted to lower limbs for >3 years
Suggests vascular parkinsonism or NPH. Upper limb involvement appearing within 3 years does NOT trigger this exclusion. Must be continuous and unequivocal lower-body restriction throughout.
5 · Dopamine receptor blocker or depleting agent at dose/time-course consistent with drug-induced parkinsonism
Clinical judgment required. Low-dose highly-atypical neuroleptic (e.g., low-dose quetiapine for sleep) may not qualify. If parkinsonism clearly persists long after complete withdrawal, the blocker may have unmasked subclinical PD.
6 · No observable response to high-dose levodopa despite at least moderate severity
Requires ≥600 mg/day levodopa — cannot apply if untreated or on <600 mg. Patient must have at least moderate severity (UPDRS rigidity or bradykinesia >2 on at least one measure). Absence of response confirmed by reliable patient/witness or objectively (≤3-point improvement on UPDRS-III). Tremor-predominant PD may respond less — evaluate rigidity/bradykinesia subscores.
7 · Unequivocal cortical sensory loss, clear limb ideomotor apraxia, or progressive aphasia
Cortical sensory: graphesthesia, stereognosis with intact primary modalities. Clear limb ideomotor apraxia. Progressive aphasia. Must be unequivocal. Mild visuospatial difficulty or executive dysfunction in established PD does not qualify. Primary concern: CBS.
8 · Normal functional neuroimaging of presynaptic dopaminergic system (if performed)
If not performed, this criterion does NOT apply. Definitively normal DaTscan or 18F-DOPA PET excludes PD. Equivocal reads do not trigger this exclusion.
Scope: DaTscan identifies nigrostriatal degeneration vs. non-parkinsonism mimics (ET, dystonic tremor) — it does NOT differentiate PD from other degenerative parkinsonisms (MSA, PSP, CBS). An abnormal DaTscan is therefore NOT a supportive criterion in Step 4.
9 · Alternative condition plausibly causing parkinsonism, or expert believes alternative syndrome more likely than PD
Includes rare mimics (Wilson disease, HMSN, hypoparathyroidism) and common alternative parkinsonian syndromes (MSA, PSP, CBS). DLB is NOT an alternative syndrome under this criterion. Also captures the expert clinician's overall gestalt.
Step 3 · Red Flags
Each red flag must be counterbalanced by a supportive criterion. More than 2 red flags → Probable PD cannot be diagnosed.
Diagnostic logic (Postuma 2015, Table 1):
Clinically Established PD — No red flags + ≥2 supportive
Clinically Probable PD — 1 RF → ≥1 supportive; 2 RF → ≥2 supportive; max 2 red flags
In all cases: number of supportive criteria ≥ number of red flags
1 · Rapid progression to regular wheelchair use within 5 years of onset
Regular (not episodic) wheelchair use. Wheelchair use from unrelated cause (orthopedic injury, spinal cord injury) does not apply. PSP-RS and MSA-P are primary concerns.
2 · Complete absence of motor progression over ≥5 years (unless treatment-related)
Must be based on direct observation — historical information alone is insufficient. Absence must be continuous over ≥5 years. Stability clearly attributable to adequate dopaminergic therapy does NOT trigger this red flag.
3 · Early severe bulbar dysfunction within first 5 years (severe dysphonia/dysarthria or severe dysphagia requiring soft food/NGT/PEG)
UPDRS severity thresholds: dysarthria ≥4, dysphagia ≥3. Hypophonia and mild hyparthria are common in early PD and do NOT qualify. Targets frank bulbar failure suggesting MSA or PSP.
4 · Inspiratory respiratory dysfunction — diurnal or nocturnal inspiratory stridor or frequent inspiratory sighs
Distinct from obstructive sleep apnea. Nocturnal vocal cord abductor palsy is characteristic of MSA. Frequent involuntary inspiratory sighs as a distinct respiratory pattern also qualifies.
5 · Severe autonomic failure within first 5 years (orthostatic hypotension or severe urinary dysfunction)
(a) OH: ≥30 mmHg systolic or ≥15 mmHg diastolic within 3 min of standing, in the absence of dehydration, medication, or other plausible causes. (b) Severe urinary incontinence or retention within first 5 years — in men not from prostate disease, must be associated with erectile dysfunction. Excludes long-standing stress incontinence in women.
Mild autonomic dysfunction is common in PD and does not qualify. This criterion targets severe autonomic failure as seen particularly in MSA. If disease duration exceeds 5 years, features must have been documented within the first 5 years.
6 · Recurrent falls (>1/year) due to impaired balance within 3 years of onset
Falls attributable to balance impairment. Excludes falls from syncope/seizure or situations where normal-balance individuals would also fall. Clinical judgment required to determine whether balance impairment was the key factor.
7 · Disproportionate anterocollis (dystonic) or hand/foot contractures within first 10 years
Dystonic in nature and disproportionate to disease stage. Distinguish from levodopa-induced off-state foot dystonia (consistent with PD). Severe camptocormia or Pisa syndrome appearing in the first decade and not attributable to levodopa therapy.
8 · Absence of ANY common nonmotor PD features despite ≥5 years of disease
All of the following must be absent: sleep dysfunction (insomnia, EDS, RBD symptoms), autonomic dysfunction (constipation, daytime urinary urgency, symptomatic orthostasis), hyposmia, psychiatric dysfunction (depression, anxiety, hallucinations). Only apply after full 5 years of disease.
9 · Unexplained pyramidal tract signs — pyramidal weakness or clear pathologic hyperreflexia
Excludes: mild reflex asymmetry in the more affected limb (common in PD) and isolated extensor plantar response (may reflect striatal toe or mild cervical myelopathy). Unequivocal clonus, Babinski, or pyramidal weakness qualifies.
10 · Bilateral symmetric parkinsonism throughout the entire disease course
Patient or caregiver reports bilateral symptom onset with NO side predominance, AND no side predominance observed on objective examination. PD should demonstrate persistent asymmetry. Treatment-related improvement in asymmetry does not trigger this red flag.

Step 4 · Supportive Criteria
4 criteria per MDS-PD criteria. Each met criterion increases diagnostic confidence and counterbalances one red flag.
0/ 4 Supportive
0/ 10 Red Flags
—
1 · Clear and dramatic beneficial response to dopaminergic therapy
Patient returned to normal or near-normal function with initial treatment. If initial response not documented: (a) Marked improvement with dose increases or marked worsening with dose decreases — documented objectively (>30% UPDRS-III improvement) or clearly by reliable patient/caregiver; OR (b) Unequivocal on/off fluctuations with predictable end-of-dose wearing off.
Some response is insufficient — must be unequivocal and of large amplitude. Modest response does not meet criteria. Predictable wearing off confirms true dopaminergic fluctuations (not day-to-day variability). Documentation can be retrospective.
2 · Levodopa-induced dyskinesia
Peak-dose choreiform or dystonic movements clearly related to dopaminergic drug timing. Biphasic (diphasic) dyskinesia also qualifies. Must be clearly drug-related and not attributable to another hyperkinetic disorder.
3 · Rest tremor of a limb, documented on clinical examination (past or current)
Must be documented on clinical examination — current exam or clearly recorded in a prior examination. Included because: (1) rest tremor is less common in atypical parkinsonism; (2) tremor-predominant PD may respond less to levodopa, making Criterion #1 harder to meet.
4 · Ancillary test with >80% specificity — olfactory loss (hyposmic/anosmic range) OR abnormal cardiac MIBG scintigraphy
Single supportive criterion — met by either or both: (a) Olfactory loss in anosmic or clearly hyposmic range, adjusted for age and sex on formal testing (UPSIT, Sniffin' Sticks); (b) MIBG scintigraphy with reduced late H/M ratio clearly documenting cardiac sympathetic denervation. Cannot be counted twice if both positive.
DaTscan does NOT qualify — it differentiates parkinsonism from non-parkinsonism mimics (ET, dystonic tremor), not PD from other degenerative parkinsonisms. An abnormal DaTscan should NOT be entered here. Only olfactory testing and MIBG meet the MDS threshold of >80% specificity in ≥3 independent multicenter studies.
Step 5 · Ancillary Testing Reference
Criteria entries belong in Steps 2–4. This step provides protocol and interpretation references.
Key principles: MDS-PD criteria are applicable without ancillary testing. · Olfactory testing / MIBG → Supportive Criterion #4 · Normal DaTscan → Absolute Exclusion #8 · Abnormal DaTscan confirms nigrostriatal degeneration but does NOT differentiate PD from MSA/PSP/CBS
Formal Acute Levodopa Challenge (objective documentation of Supportive Criterion #1): 1. Withhold morning dopaminergic medications (≥12h for short-acting agents) 2. Score MDS-UPDRS Part III — baseline "off" state (same examiner pre/post) 3. Administer levodopa/carbidopa 250/25 mg; use 375/37.5 mg if weight >80 kg 4. Pre-treat with domperidone 20 mg PO 30 min prior if levodopa-naive 5. Rescore UPDRS-III at 45, 60, and 90 min post-dose 6. % improvement = [(baseline − best post-dose) / baseline] × 100 7. ≥30% objective improvement on UPDRS-III → Criterion #1 met OR unequivocal on/off fluctuations with predictable wearing off → also meets Criterion #1 Notes: - Tremor-predominant PD may show <30% improvement even in true PD → Evaluate rigidity/bradykinesia subscores independently - A negative acute challenge does not exclude PD - Only a trial at ≥600 mg/day is required to apply Absolute Exclusion #6
Structural MRI red flag signs (not part of MDS-PD criteria): MSA-P / MSA-C: · Putaminal rim sign — T2/FLAIR hypointensity posterolateral putamen with hyperintense rim · Hot cross bun sign — cruciform T2 hyperintensity in pons (MSA-C) · Middle cerebellar peduncle and cerebellar atrophy PSP: · Hummingbird/morning glory sign — midbrain atrophy on sagittal T1 · Midbrain-to-pons area ratio <0.52 (validated vs. PD) · Superior cerebellar peduncle atrophy CBS: · Asymmetric posterior frontal/parietal cortical atrophy (contralateral to more affected side) Vascular Parkinsonism: · Bilateral periventricular/subcortical WMH (Fazekas ≥2) · Lacunar infarcts in basal ganglia or internal capsule Emerging (not criteria-validated): · Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI — reduced SNc signal · Free water in posterior putamen (PD vs. atypical)
123I-MIBG Myocardial Scintigraphy: Interpretation: · Late H/M ratio <1.7–2.2 in PD/DLB (varies by camera/protocol) · Reduced uptake = cardiac sympathetic denervation → supports Lewy body pathology · Specificity >80% for PD/DLB vs. MSA, PSP, vascular parkinsonism Medications that must be withheld before scan (reduce uptake — false positive): · Labetalol, carvedilol · Tricyclic antidepressants · Opioids Other false positive causes: · Diabetic autonomic neuropathy · Congestive heart failure / ischemic cardiomyopathy · Amyloid cardiomyopathy False negatives (normal MIBG despite PD): · Early disease — denervation may be incomplete · LRRK2-PD — often preserves cardiac MIBG (variable Lewy body burden) Entry: Abnormal MIBG → mark Supportive Criterion #4 (Step 4) as Present Normal MIBG → does not exclude PD, but reduces Lewy body probability

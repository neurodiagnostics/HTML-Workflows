Step 1 · Select Suspected Subtype
Narcolepsy Type 1 is defined by cataplexy and/or orexin (hypocretin) deficiency. Type 2 is diagnosed in the absence of cataplexy with documented MSLT criteria. The pathway will adapt accordingly.
ICSD-3-TR (2023) key update for Type 1: Previously, Criterion B required cataplexy with MSLT findings (with an overnight SOREMP able to substitute for one MSLT SOREMP). Based on now well-established evidence, cataplexy alone + a single overnight PSG SOREMP (within 15 min of sleep onset) is now sufficient to fulfill Criterion B — even without an MSLT demonstrating ≥2 SOREMPs.
Step 2 · Criterion A — Excessive Daytime Sleepiness
Criterion A is identical for both narcolepsy subtypes and is the mandatory cardinal symptom of narcolepsy.
Criterion A REQUIRED — BOTH SUBTYPES
Daily periods of irrepressible need to sleep, or daytime lapses into drowsiness or sleep
Excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) is the universal, defining symptom of narcolepsy. The irrepressible quality distinguishes narcoleptic sleep attacks from voluntary drowsiness — patients cannot resist the urge to sleep. Sleep attacks may be brief (minutes), often refreshing in Type 1 (in contrast to idiopathic hypersomnia, where naps are typically unrefreshing). EDS must be distinguished from fatigue (the subjective sense of energy depletion without true sleepiness).
Type 2 duration requirement: For Narcolepsy Type 2 only, Criterion A specifies EDS occurring for ≥3 months. Type 1 has no minimum duration specified in the ICSD-3-TR criteria (though EDS is typically present for a prolonged period before diagnosis).

Supporting tools: Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) score ≥10 suggests pathological EDS. The Stanford Sleepiness Scale and patient diaries can supplement the history. Sleep logs + actigraphy (whenever possible) are required prior to in-laboratory sleep testing to evaluate for insufficient sleep and circadian rhythm disturbances — a critical pre-MSLT step.
Type 2 duration qualifier ≥3 MONTHS REQUIRED
EDS present for ≥3 months (Type 2 specific)
Narcolepsy Type 2 requires documentation of EDS for at least 3 months before the diagnosis can be made. This minimum duration requirement helps exclude transient hypersomnolence from acute illness, medication effects, or situational sleep deprivation.
Step 3 · Criterion B — Type 1 Diagnostic Evidence
Criterion B is met by either Branch 1 (cataplexy + MSLT/PSG) OR Branch 2 (CSF orexin deficiency). Either pathway independently fulfills the diagnosis.
Type 1 Criterion B requires ONE of:
Branch 1: Cataplexy + MSLT (MSL ≤8 min + ≥2 SOREMPs) OR cataplexy + overnight PSG SOREMP (within 15 min of sleep onset) ← New in ICSD-3-TR 2023
Branch 2: CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL (Stanford reference) or <⅓ of mean normal values — if positive, diagnosis confirmed regardless of other comorbidities
Branch 1 · Cataplexy + Sleep Study Evidence EITHER BRANCH SUFFICIENT
Cataplexy — sudden bilateral loss of muscle tone precipitated by strong emotions, in a conscious patient
Typical cataplexy (ICSD-3-TR "Essential Features"):
Sudden, reversible episodes of bilateral skeletal muscle weakness or atonia triggered by strong positive emotions — laughter is the most common trigger; also humor, surprise, excitement, pride, anger. The patient remains conscious and aware during the episode. Duration: seconds to a few minutes. Spontaneous full recovery.

Manifestations range from subtle (jaw sagging, head drop, knee buckling, facial drooping) to complete postural collapse. Partial episodes are more common than complete. Respiratory and ocular muscles are typically spared.

Atypical cataplexy: Triggered by negative emotions (fear, frustration) or occurring spontaneously. More common in secondary narcolepsy (post-encephalitic, hypothalamic tumor, NMO). A higher index of suspicion for alternative diagnoses is warranted when only atypical cataplexy is present.
Differential: Pseudocataplexy (syncope — pallor, tachycardia, loss of consciousness, non-emotional triggers), drop attacks (no emotional trigger, no retained consciousness), startle disease (exaggerated startle, not emotion-triggered), epileptic drop attacks (typically no emotional trigger, post-ictal confusion), sudden loss of postural tone in REM sleep disorder.

Branch 2 · CSF Orexin (Hypocretin-1) INDEPENDENT OF BRANCH 1
CSF hypocretin-1 ≤110 pg/mL (Stanford reference) or <⅓ of mean normal values with the same standardized assay
Measured by radioimmunoassay. Two thresholds are accepted: (1) absolute: ≤110 pg/mL using the Stanford reference sample — the original validated cutoff; (2) relative: <⅓ of mean control values using the same standardized assay — allows for laboratory-specific calibration.

Pathophysiology: ~70,000–90,000 hypothalamic orexin (hypocretin) neurons are selectively destroyed in Type 1, almost certainly by autoimmune mechanisms (strong HLA-DQB1*06:02 association). CSF orexin levels reflect this loss — typically <50 pg/mL in florid Type 1 (often undetectable).
ICSD-3-TR note: If orexin deficiency is verified, narcolepsy Type 1 should be diagnosed regardless of other comorbidities that could potentially be related to the clinical symptoms — the finding is definitive.

HLA testing: HLA-DQB1*06:02 is present in ~95% of Type 1 (vs. ~25% of general population). A negative HLA-DQB1*06:02 makes orexin deficiency extremely unlikely — consider testing before LP to guide pre-test probability. A positive HLA does not diagnose narcolepsy.

Indications for LP: Atypical cataplexy, MSLT borderline, secondary narcolepsy suspected (trauma, tumor, inflammatory), or when diagnosis requires certainty for disability or pharmacotherapy decisions.
Step 3 · Criteria B–D — Type 2 Diagnostic Evidence
Type 2 is a diagnosis of exclusion relative to Type 1. All of Criteria B, C, and D must be satisfied. Cataplexy must be absent and orexin must not be deficient.
Type 2 requires ALL of:
Criterion B: MSLT — MSL ≤8 min + ≥2 SOREMPs (overnight PSG SOREMP may replace 1 MSLT SOREMP — diagnostic value not established for Type 2, but guides clinical decision-making)
Criterion C: Cataplexy absent
Criterion D: CSF hypocretin-1 normal (>110 pg/mL) if measured — or not measured
Criterion B · MSLT REQUIRED
Mean sleep latency ≤8 minutes on MSLT
MSLT performed following a nocturnal PSG (to confirm ≥6h of sleep, exclude OSA/PLMs causing EDS). Five 20-min nap opportunities every 2 hours. Mean of all nap latencies calculated. Must be performed after ensuring no medication washout effects and following sleep logs/actigraphy confirming adequate sleep opportunity.
ICSD-3-TR note: Narcolepsy Type 2 should not be diagnosed in a shift worker without prior re-entrainment to a normal sleep-wake schedule — the circadian clock gates REM propensity and can produce false-positive SOREMPs in shift work disorder.
≥2 SOREMPs on MSLT (or 1 MSLT SOREMP + overnight PSG SOREMP)
Sleep-onset REM period = REM sleep occurring within 15 minutes of sleep onset. On MSLT: 2 or more SOREMPs across the 5 nap opportunities. Alternatively, the overnight PSG SOREMP (within 15 min of sleep onset) may replace one MSLT SOREMP — though ICSD-3-TR notes the diagnostic value of this substitution is not formally established for Type 2 (unlike Type 1 where it is now a recognized independent criterion).
Criterion C · Absence of Cataplexy REQUIRED
Cataplexy is absent
True cataplexy must be absent for a Type 2 diagnosis. If cataplexy develops at any subsequent point, the diagnosis must be reclassified as Narcolepsy Type 1. A careful history for emotion-triggered partial episodes (jaw drop, head nod, knee buckle, facial droop, slurred speech) should be obtained — these may not be spontaneously reported.
Criterion D · CSF Orexin (if measured) ONLY IF TESTED
If CSF hypocretin-1 measured: must be normal (>110 pg/mL or >⅓ mean normal values)
If CSF orexin has been measured and is deficient (≤110 pg/mL or <⅓ mean), the diagnosis must be reclassified as Narcolepsy Type 1 — even if cataplexy is currently absent (atypical presentation or early Type 1 before cataplexy develops). LP is not required for Type 2 diagnosis — if not measured, this criterion is simply not applicable.
Step 4 · Criterion C/E — Exclusion of Alternative Causes
The final criterion is identical in both subtypes: symptoms must not be better explained by any of the following conditions.
Exclusion criterion (Criterion C for Type 1, Criterion E for Type 2) ALL MUST BE ADDRESSED
Chronic insufficient sleep excluded
Sleep deprivation is the most common cause of EDS and can produce false-positive MSLT findings including SOREMPs, particularly in adolescents. Sleep logs and actigraphy for ≥1–2 weeks prior to MSLT are mandatory to document adequate sleep opportunity. A sleep diary showing consistently <7 hours/night in an adult, or habitually curtailed sleep, should prompt sleep extension trials before proceeding to MSLT.
Adolescents with delayed sleep phase commonly accumulate sleep debt on school nights. Extended sleep (e.g., weekend "catch-up" or vacation sleep) should normalize ESS scores and MSLT findings in pure chronic insufficient sleep — failure to normalize after adequate sleep opportunity supports a primary hypersomnolence disorder.
Circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorder excluded
Delayed sleep phase disorder (DSPD) — especially in adolescents and young adults — can cause profound EDS on a conventional schedule and false-positive SOREMPs on an MSLT performed at an inappropriate circadian time. Shift work disorder similarly gates REM propensity. Sleep logs + actigraphy confirm timing of habitual sleep-wake cycle. MSLT must be performed at the appropriate circadian time relative to the individual's habitual schedule.
Other current sleep disorder excluded (e.g., OSA, idiopathic hypersomnia, REM sleep behavior disorder)
Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA): Must be adequately treated before MSLT — residual sleepiness from untreated or undertreated OSA can cause short sleep latencies and SOREMPs. If CPAP therapy fully resolves EDS, a primary hypersomnolence disorder is not supported.

Idiopathic hypersomnia (IH): MSLT findings in IH typically show MSL ≤8 min but <2 SOREMPs. IH is explicitly listed as a differential for narcolepsy — both Type 1 and Type 2 MSLT criteria (ICSD-3-TR) now require that MSLT/PSG findings are inconsistent with narcolepsy for an IH diagnosis to be made.

Other: PLMs causing fragmented sleep, upper airway resistance syndrome, kleine-levin syndrome (episodic hypersomnia), post-COVID hypersomnolence.
Mental disorder not primarily responsible for symptoms
Major depressive disorder with hypersomnia, bipolar disorder (hypersomnia during depressive episodes), and ADHD can all cause EDS. Importantly, depression is common in narcolepsy — comorbid depression does not preclude the diagnosis. The exclusion criterion requires that EDS/cataplexy are not better explained by the psychiatric disorder — clinical judgment is required. Atonic episodes in conversion disorder/functional neurological disorder may mimic cataplexy.
Medication or substance use / withdrawal not responsible for symptoms
Drugs causing EDS or MSLT abnormalities: sedating antihistamines, benzodiazepines, opioids, anticonvulsants, antipsychotics, antidepressants (especially TCAs, mirtazapine), and cannabis. REM-suppressing medications (TCAs, SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs) — withdrawal can cause REM rebound and false-positive SOREMPs. Standard guidance: allow ≥2 weeks washout of REM-altering medications before MSLT if clinically feasible. A medication review including OTC and recreational substances is essential.

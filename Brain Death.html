⚠
Educational Use Only This tool is intended for clinical education and training purposes only. It does not replace expert clinician assessment, institutional protocol, or applicable legal requirements. It may not be used to formally determine or declare brain death / death by neurologic criteria. All BD/DNC determinations must be performed by qualified clinicians in accordance with the 2023 AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM guideline, institutional policy, and state law.
Step 1 · Prerequisites for BD/DNC Evaluation
All prerequisites must be satisfied before initiating the BD/DNC evaluation. Each item must be confirmed. A single unmet prerequisite requires deferral or alternative approach.
Guiding principle (Rec. 7a, Level A): BD/DNC evaluation can only be initiated when a patient has sustained a catastrophic, permanent brain injury caused by an identified mechanism known to lead to BD/DNC. This presumption of non-brain-death must be actively disproved through a standardized process.
A · Etiology & Neuroimaging ALL MANDATORY
Identified catastrophic brain injury with known mechanism consistent with BD/DNC
A definitive etiology must be established that is known to cause BD/DNC. Accepted causes include: traumatic brain injury, subarachnoid hemorrhage, intracerebral hemorrhage, massive ischemic stroke, hypoxic-ischemic brain injury (HIBI, e.g. post-cardiac arrest), intracranial infection with malignant cerebral edema, and space-occupying lesions causing herniation. Early after injury, imaging findings may not yet demonstrate full severity; repeat neuroimaging can document progression. If no mechanism is identified, BD/DNC evaluation must not proceed.
Reversible mimics that can produce clinical BD/DNC appearance: Guillain-Barré syndrome, leptomeningeal carcinomatosis, snake envenomation, botulism, high cervical cord injuries, severe metabolic encephalopathy. These must be excluded by clinical context and workup (Rec. 7b, Level A).
Neuroimaging consistent with severe, irreversible brain injury
CT or MRI must demonstrate an acute CNS injury consistent with the profound loss of brain function (2010 AAN; reaffirmed 2023). For primary infratentorial injury (e.g., posterior fossa hemorrhage or infarct): the 2023 guideline requires confirmation of catastrophic supratentorial injury on neuroimaging before initiating the BD/DNC evaluation, to meet the UDDA requirement of loss of function of the brain as a whole. If isolated infratentorial pathology is present, ancillary testing demonstrating absent cerebral blood flow is recommended even if clinical exam and apnea test are consistent with BD/DNC.
Primary infratentorial injury — special consideration: Ancillary testing to demonstrate absent cerebral blood flow is recommended regardless of clinical exam result (Neurology CPJ 2023; World Brain Death Project 2020).
B · Observation Period & Environment MANDATORY
Adequate observation period to confirm severity and permanency
The patient must be observed for sufficient time to determine the severity and permanency of the brain injury and to exclude confounders (Rec. 4, Level A). The 2023 guideline does not mandate a specific numeric waiting period for adults but requires that the permanency of injury be established before evaluation. For children: additional minimum observation intervals apply (not covered in this adult-focused tool). Clinical judgment — guided by the acuity and mechanism of injury — determines adequacy. The evaluation must occur in an environment with staff trained to manage the cardiopulmonary complications of apnea testing.
No legislated mandatory waiting period exists in most US states for adults. Institutional policy may add requirements. Ensure hemodynamic stability and resuscitation to physiologic targets before proceeding.
C · Examiner Qualifications MANDATORY
Examiner is credentialed, trained, and competent in BD/DNC evaluation
Attending clinicians performing BD/DNC examinations must be appropriately credentialed members of the hospital's medical staff and adequately trained and competent in the evaluation of BD/DNC in adults (Rec. 6a, Level A). Eligible specialties include: intensivists, neurologists, neurosurgeons, and others per institutional credentialing. APPs may perform BD/DNC evaluations independently only if permitted by local law and institutional standards and if appropriately credentialed (Rec. 6b, Level A). Trainees must be directly supervised at the bedside by a qualifying attending for the entirety of the evaluation (Rec. 6c, Level A).
Conflict of interest (Rec. 3, Level A): Any clinician involved in surgical recovery of organs for transplantation must NOT be involved in BD/DNC evaluation. BD/DNC examiners must consider only the interests of their patient.
Step 2 · Exclude Confounders
All reversible conditions that could mimic or interfere with BD/DNC must be corrected or excluded. Any unresolved confounder invalidates the clinical examination.
Key principle: Conditions such as metabolic derangements, intoxication, or medications can depress the CNS and result in a comatose state with absent brainstem reflexes that is reversible. These must be systematically excluded before the neurologic examination and apnea test are considered valid. Document explicitly in the medical record.
A · Temperature MUST CORRECT
Core temperature ≥36°C (96.8°F)
Hypothermia may blunt or abolish brainstem reflexes independent of brain injury. The 2023 AAN guideline requires that temperature be at or above 36°C before proceeding. Active rewarming to this threshold is required in hypothermic patients. In patients managed with targeted temperature management (TTM) post-cardiac arrest: the 2023 guideline recommends that BD/DNC evaluation not begin until at least 24 hours after rewarming to ≥36°C (though TTM itself does not preclude subsequent BD/DNC determination once rewarmed).
For TTM patients: allow adequate washout time from sedatives and neuromuscular blockers used during TTM before proceeding.
B · Hemodynamics MUST CORRECT
Systolic BP ≥100 mmHg (adults) or MAP ≥75 mmHg
Hypotension may suppress brain function independently of injury. Vasopressors and fluids should be optimized to achieve hemodynamic stability before evaluation. The apnea test abort threshold during the test is SBP ≤100 mmHg or MAP ≤75 mmHg in adults despite vasopressor/inotrope support (eTable 2, 2023 guideline supplement). ECMO does not preclude BD/DNC evaluation; specialized techniques (CO₂ blending into oxygenator) are available for apnea testing in ECMO patients.
ECMO consideration: Venoarterial (VA) ECMO supports circulation but does not independently produce brain function. Specialized apnea testing protocols apply — consult the 2023 guideline supplement.
C · CNS-Depressant Drugs & Neuromuscular Blockade MUST EXCLUDE
No residual effect of CNS depressants, sedatives, anesthetics, or neuromuscular blockers
CNS depressants (barbiturates, benzodiazepines, opioids, propofol, dexmedetomidine, ketamine) and neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs) can all produce reversible clinical findings mimicking BD/DNC. All must have adequate time for metabolism/excretion, taking into account altered hepatic and renal function in critically ill patients. For barbiturates: serum levels should be checked; therapeutic coma from barbiturates may require ancillary testing. For NMBAs: train-of-four (TOF) testing must confirm 4/4 twitches before proceeding. Blood toxicology screening is recommended if drug ingestion is suspected.
High-dose barbiturate coma: Clinical examination is unreliable even at sub-therapeutic levels. Ancillary cerebral blood flow testing is recommended/required in this scenario.
D · Metabolic Derangements MUST CORRECT
No severe electrolyte, acid-base, or endocrine disturbance
The 2023 guideline acknowledges that determination of specific cut-off laboratory values is arbitrary, but lists metabolic derangements that warrant correction before proceeding (eTable 1). These include: severe hyponatremia or hypernatremia, severe hypocalcemia or hypercalcemia, severe hypophosphatemia, severe hypermagnesemia, severe hepatic failure (hyperammonemia), severe hypothyroidism (myxedema coma), Addisonian crisis, and severe acidosis or alkalosis not attributable to injury.
Ammonia and thyroid function tests are NOT routinely measured but should be obtained if encephalopathy of unclear etiology.
PaO₂ ≥200 mmHg on 100% FiO₂ (pre-apnea test oxygenation)
Before initiating the apnea test, the patient must be pre-oxygenated with 100% FiO₂ for 10 minutes to achieve a PaO₂ ≥200 mmHg. This provides an adequate oxygen reservoir during the apnea period to prevent hypoxemia before the PaCO₂ threshold is reached. A baseline ABG should be drawn at the start of apnea testing to document PaCO₂. This is an apnea-test-specific prerequisite and is assessed here to allow planning.
Preoxygenation target: PaO₂ >200 mmHg ensures ~10 minutes of apnea time before SpO₂ drops below 85% in most patients — sufficient for PaCO₂ to rise to ≥60 mmHg.
Step 3 · Neurologic Examination
All components must be absent for the examination to be consistent with BD/DNC. Any single preserved response indicates brain or brainstem function and is inconsistent with BD/DNC.
Core concept (Rec. 5, Level A): Spontaneous breathing, absence of coma, any intact brainstem reflex, or motor activity other than spinally mediated reflexes indicate brain function. BD/DNC cannot be declared if any component is present or equivocal. The examination focuses on demonstrating loss of all brainstem reflexes, as complete loss of the brainstem reticular activating system is the most robust predictor of permanence.
A · Consciousness & Motor Response ALL ABSENT REQUIRED
Deep coma — no response to noxious stimuli
No evidence of consciousness or awareness. Patients must show no purposeful or reflexive motor response to noxious stimulation anywhere on the body. Apply noxious stimuli to the head/face (supraorbital pressure, TMJ pressure) and trunk/limbs. The GCS motor score must be 1 (no response), not decorticate or decerebrate posturing, and not withdrawal to painful stimuli.
Spinal reflexes: Spinally-mediated movements (Lazarus sign, triple flexion, limb movements to stimulation) may occur after BD/DNC and do NOT indicate brain function. They represent intact spinal cord reflex arcs and must be distinguished from brainstem-mediated responses.
No brain/brainstem-mediated motor movements
No decerebrate or decorticate posturing. No purposeful movements. No seizures. The 2023 guideline includes a table of spinal reflexes adapted from the World Brain Death Project (JAMA 2020) to assist in distinguishing spinal from brainstem-mediated movements. Examples of acceptable spinal reflexes: Babinski sign, triple flexion, finger jerks, undulating toe sign, arm-raising (Lazarus sign). These do not preclude BD/DNC determination.
B · Pupillary Reflex MUST BE ABSENT
Absent pupillary light reflex bilaterally — pupils fixed (typically mid-position or dilated)
Both pupils must be non-reactive to bright light. Pupils are typically 4–9 mm (mid-position to dilated) in BD/DNC. A bright, focused light source should be used. Assess direct and consensual responses bilaterally. The pupillary light reflex arc involves CN II (afferent) and CN III (efferent via ciliary ganglion); bilateral absence indicates midbrain dysfunction. Asymmetric or unequal pupils alone are not sufficient — both must be completely non-reactive.
Caveats: Prior mydriatics (atropine, phenylephrine drops), severe miosis from opioid overdose (may prevent dilation), ocular trauma, or pre-existing Adie's tonic pupil may confound assessment. Document bilateral testing with a bright light source explicitly.
C · Oculomotor Reflexes BOTH ABSENT REQUIRED
Absent oculocephalic reflex (doll's eyes)
Rapidly turn the head side-to-side and up/down. Eyes should remain fixed (no conjugate deviation in the opposite direction). Tests the MLF, PPRF, and CN III/VI via vestibular pathways. In BD/DNC, eyes move passively with the head (no counterrolling). This reflex may be OMITTED if there is cervical spine instability or injury — document reason for omission. If omitted, the oculovestibular reflex becomes more critical.
C-spine injury: If oculocephalic reflex cannot be safely performed, the oculovestibular reflex must be performed bilaterally and may require ancillary testing if equivocal.
Absent oculovestibular reflex (cold caloric) bilaterally
Inspect ear canals for occlusion or TM perforation. With HOB at 30°, instill ≥30 mL of ice-cold water slowly into one ear, observe for 60 seconds. Allow 5 minutes between sides. In BD/DNC: no deviation of the eyes toward the irrigated ear (absent tonic deviation). Tests CN VIII, brainstem vestibular nuclei, MLF, and CN III/VI. This is typically the more sensitive test of the two oculomotor reflexes.
Bilateral absence required. Sedatives (especially benzodiazepines) can suppress caloric responses. Cerumen occlusion or perforated TM may prevent adequate testing — document and consider ancillary testing if both ears are untestable.
D · Corneal, Gag & Cough Reflexes ALL ABSENT REQUIRED
Absent corneal reflex bilaterally
Lightly touch each cornea with a cotton wisp or cotton swab — observe for any eyelid movement (blink). Tests CN V (afferent: ophthalmic branch) and CN VII (efferent: orbicularis oculi). Contact lens use can reduce corneal sensitivity; this should be noted. Bilateral absence indicates bilateral CN V and/or VII nuclear or nerve dysfunction. Direct eye injury may confound the ipsilateral response.
Absent gag reflex
Stimulate the posterior pharynx bilaterally with a Yankauer suction catheter or tongue depressor. Observe for any pharyngeal or palatal contraction. Tests CN IX (afferent: glossopharyngeal) and CN X (efferent: vagus). In intubated patients, this test must still be performed — gently stimulate the pharynx around the ETT. Suctioning the back of the throat on both sides is the preferred technique per 2023 guideline supplement (eTable).
In BD/DNC, there should be no pharyngeal movement whatsoever. Even partial response (slight palatal twitch) is inconsistent with BD/DNC.
Absent cough reflex to deep tracheal suctioning
Pass a suction catheter deep into the trachea via the ETT and apply suction. Observe for any cough effort or bronchospasm. Tests CN X and medullary cough center. In BD/DNC: no cough or respiratory effort, no chest wall movement. This test also provides early information about respiratory drive that will be formally assessed in the apnea test. Deep suctioning (to carina) provides the strongest stimulus.
The cough reflex test and apnea test both assess medullary function. A positive cough to suctioning (any respiratory effort) is inconsistent with BD/DNC and makes a positive apnea test unlikely.

Step 4 · Apnea Test
The apnea test is a mandatory component of BD/DNC determination. Minimum one apnea test is required in adults (after the final neurologic examination). It assesses medullary respiratory center function by challenging respiratory drive with hypercarbia and acidosis.
Physiologic basis: Medullary chemoreceptors are stimulated by rising PaCO₂ and falling pH. In BD/DNC, there is no respiratory response despite profound hypercarbia. The PaCO₂ target of ≥60 mmHg AND ≥20 mmHg above pre-apnea baseline (with pH <7.30) provides a maximal adequate stimulus. The rate of PaCO₂ rise averages ~3 mmHg/min but is highly variable (0.5–10.5 mmHg/min); ABGs should be checked every 2 minutes.
A · Apnea Test Technique SEQUENTIAL STEPS
1
Pre-oxygenate with 100% FiO₂ for 10 minutes. Adjust ventilator to achieve PaCO₂ at the patient's known baseline (or ~35–45 mmHg if unknown) and pH 7.35–7.45. Draw baseline ABG.
2
If PEEP is high, gradually reduce to ≤5 cmH₂O as tolerated. Ensure hemodynamics are acceptable (SBP ≥100 mmHg, MAP ≥75 mmHg). Continue vasopressors as needed.
3
Disconnect from ventilator (or switch to T-piece/CPAP). Deliver O₂ at 6 L/min via catheter through ETT (or maintain 100% FiO₂ by CPAP/T-piece). Monitor SpO₂, HR, BP, ECG continuously.
4
Observe closely for any respiratory movement (nasal flare, intercostal muscle use, abdominal breathing, diaphragm contraction). Even a single breath = test negative = not BD/DNC.
5
Draw ABG every 2 minutes. Continue until PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg AND ≥20 mmHg above baseline, AND pH <7.30 (in patients without chronic CO₂ retention). This typically requires 8–10 minutes.
6
If no respiratory effort is observed at target PaCO₂/pH: apnea test is POSITIVE (consistent with BD/DNC). Reconnect ventilator. Obtain final ABG before reconnection if possible.
B · Apnea Test Result REQUIRED
Apnea test result: No respiratory effort at adequate PaCO₂ target
Target criteria for positive apnea test (2023 AAN guideline, eTable 2):

Standard patient (no chronic CO₂ retention): PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg AND ≥20 mmHg above pre-apnea baseline, AND pH <7.30, with no respiratory effort observed.

Known chronic CO₂ retainer with known baseline: PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg AND ≥20 mmHg above the patient's known chronic premorbid baseline.

Suspected chronic CO₂ retainer with unknown baseline: PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg AND ≥20 mmHg above pre-apnea test level — however, ancillary testing is required in addition.
Abort criteria (immediately reconnect ventilator): SpO₂ <85%; SBP ≤100 mmHg or MAP ≤75 mmHg in adults despite vasopressors; cardiac arrhythmia with hemodynamic instability. If aborted before reaching target PaCO₂ and patient was stable, repeat after re-preoxygenation OR perform ancillary test.
C · Chronic CO₂ Retention Status (for apnea test interpretation)
Patient's chronic CO₂ retention status
This determines the applicable PaCO₂ threshold for the apnea test. Patients with COPD, obesity hypoventilation syndrome, or other causes of chronic hypercapnia have a higher baseline PaCO₂ and therefore require a higher absolute PaCO₂ target (≥20 mmHg above their known premorbid baseline). If the baseline is unknown, the standard threshold (≥60 mmHg AND ≥20 mmHg above pre-test level) applies, but ancillary testing is required to confirm BD/DNC.
Step 5 · Ancillary Testing
Ancillary testing is NOT required if the clinical examination and apnea test can be safely and fully completed without confounders. It is required only when the clinical assessment cannot be completed or is unreliable.
Key principle: BD/DNC determination is based on clinical assessment. Ancillary testing is required only when (1) confounders cannot be fully excluded, (2) the apnea test is aborted or cannot be safely performed, (3) examination components are untestable (e.g., facial trauma, bilateral TM rupture), (4) primary infratentorial injury is present, or (5) suspected chronic CO₂ retention with unknown baseline. No ancillary test is 100% sensitive or specific — each has limitations noted below.
A · Indication for Ancillary Testing ASSESS FIRST
Is ancillary testing required?
Situations requiring ancillary testing per 2023 AAN guideline:
• Any component of the neurologic examination cannot be performed or interpreted (e.g., facial/head trauma, bilateral ear canal obstruction)
• Apnea test aborted before reaching PaCO₂ target due to hemodynamic instability or hypoxemia
• Apnea test cannot be safely initiated (severe hypoxemia, severe hemodynamic instability)
• Drug effect cannot be fully excluded (e.g., high-dose barbiturate coma)
• Primary infratentorial injury — ancillary test for absent cerebral blood flow recommended even if clinical exam and apnea test are consistent with BD/DNC
• Suspected chronic CO₂ retention with unknown baseline PaCO₂
• Institutional policy or state law requires ancillary confirmation
B · Accepted Ancillary Tests (2023 AAN — Level A recommendations)
4-vessel catheter cerebral angiography — considered historical gold standard
Intra-arterial contrast injection into bilateral carotid and vertebral arteries demonstrates absent intracranial filling beyond the entry of vessels into the dura (in anterior and posterior circulations). Represents circulatory arrest from severely elevated ICP exceeding MAP. BD/DNC finding: no intracranial flow bilaterally. This is the most definitive perfusion study (Level A recommendation). Limitations: invasive, requires transport to angio suite, contrast exposure, arterial access, time-consuming.
Gold standard for cerebral circulatory arrest. Specifically assesses absence of flow bilaterally in anterior AND posterior circulations — critical for infratentorial injury assessment.
Radionuclide perfusion scintigraphy (SPECT with ⁹⁹ᵐTc-HMPAO preferred)
SPECT with a BBB-crossing agent (⁹⁹ᵐTc-HMPAO or ⁹⁹ᵐTc-ECD) is preferred; planar radionuclide angiography with a BBB-crossing agent or, if necessary, a non-BBB-crossing agent is acceptable (Level A). BD/DNC finding: absent tracer uptake in the brain — "hollow skull" or "empty lightbulb" sign on SPECT. Widely available and practical. Does not require hemodynamic stability during imaging (tracer injected at bedside; imaging can follow). Limitations: requires nuclear medicine access; open fontanelle or skull defects/craniectomy may affect results; does not assess posterior fossa well with planar technique.
Preferred over CTA or MRA by 2023 AAN guideline. SPECT is included in the accepted ancillary tests; CTA and MRA are NOT recommended by the 2023 AAN guideline due to insufficient validation.
Transcranial Doppler ultrasonography (TCD)
Widely available, non-invasive, bedside. BD/DNC findings (any one of the following in bilateral anterior AND posterior circulation vessels, documented bilaterally and on serial examinations): reverberating flow, small systolic spikes (<50 cm/s) without diastolic flow, or absent flow. Must be performed by an experienced operator. Sensitivity is lower than catheter angiography or SPECT; absent temporal windows (15–20% of patients) limit assessment. If bilateral temporal window is absent, a second ancillary test should be performed.
Limitations: Requires adequate temporal and suboccipital windows. Low sensitivity for posterior fossa — less reliable for primary infratentorial injury. Operator-dependent. The 2023 AAN guideline accepts TCD as ancillary (Level A) but acknowledges limited evidence quality.
C · Modalities No Longer Recommended NOT RECOMMENDED — 2023 AAN
⛔ EEG / Auditory Evoked Potentials (AEP) / Somatosensory Evoked Potentials (SEP) — no longer accepted ancillary tests
2023 AAN Recommendation (Level B): Do NOT use EEG, AEP, or SEP as ancillary tests to assist with the diagnosis of BD/DNC.

EEG assesses only cerebral hemisphere electrical activity — it cannot confirm absent brainstem function, which is the core requirement of BD/DNC. An isoelectric ("flat") EEG is therefore insufficient. Additional problems: EEG is highly susceptible to ICU electrical artifact, and is unreliable in the presence of sedatives (especially barbiturates) and metabolic derangements. AEPs and SEPs share similar limitations and are similarly excluded.
This is a change from prior practice. Some institutions historically used isoelectric EEG as a confirmatory ancillary test — this is explicitly not supported by the 2023 AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM consensus guideline. If your institution's protocol still lists EEG, consult your neurology/NCC service to update to the 2023 standard.

Step 1 · Clinical Context and Fundamental Principles
The 2024 AA criteria define AD as a biological disease — symptoms are not required for diagnosis. AD exists on a continuum, beginning with the appearance of biomarkers while the individual is asymptomatic.
Box 1 — Fundamental principles (carried forward from NIA-AA):
AD is defined by its biology. Detection of AD neuropathologic change (ADNPC) by biomarkers is equivalent to diagnosing the disease.
AD exists on a continuum — first evident in vivo with Core biomarkers while asymptomatic.
Symptoms are not required to diagnose AD. Unimpaired individuals with abnormal biomarkers have AD — they are not "at risk" for a disease they already have.
Clinical syndromes may be caused by disorders other than AD — clinical presentation alone is not diagnostic.
The same AD biology may result in different phenotypic presentations.
Patient context REQUIRED
Current symptomatic status
This determines the intended use of biomarker testing. The workgroup recommends against diagnostic testing in cognitively unimpaired individuals outside observational or therapeutic research contexts at this time — disease-targeted therapies are currently approved only for early symptomatic AD (MCI and mild dementia). AD can be diagnosed biologically in asymptomatic individuals, but the workgroup states it should not routinely be done for clinical purposes yet.
Five recognized AD phenotypes: amnestic (typical), language variant, visuospatial variant, behavioral variant, dysexecutive variant. Phenotype does not change the biomarker-based diagnostic criteria.
Step 2 · Core 1 Biomarkers — Diagnostic Tests for AD
An abnormal Core 1 biomarker is sufficient to establish the biological diagnosis of AD. Core 1 biomarkers become abnormal around the same time as amyloid PET — early in the disease, before symptoms.
Core 1 biomarkers (A + T1 + Hybrid Ratios):
A (Aβ proteinopathy): Amyloid PET — the reference standard
T1 (phosphorylated secreted AD tau): Plasma p-tau217 (preferred — highest accuracy in head-to-head comparisons); also p-tau181, p-tau231
Hybrid ratios (CSF): CSF Aβ42/40; CSF p-tau181/Aβ42; CSF t-tau/Aβ42

Accuracy requirement for standalone diagnosis: Minimum 90% accuracy for detecting moderate/frequent neuritic plaques at autopsy (or approved surrogate). For BBM, this means performance equivalent to approved CSF assays. Not all available plasma assays meet this threshold — only validated "accurate" assays qualify.
Amyloid PET (A biomarker) DEFINITIVE REFERENCE STANDARD
Amyloid PET — abnormal (positive for moderate/frequent neuritic plaques)
Approved tracers (florbetapir, flutemetamol, florbetaben): visual reads have sensitivity ~96%, specificity ~100% for CERAD moderate/frequent neuritic plaques vs. neuropathologic reference within 1 year. Quantification using Centiloid scale is the accepted standardized method. Amyloid PET positive → ~95% of symptomatic individuals will have intermediate/high ADNPC (Braak III–VI); ~74–87% of asymptomatic individuals at that time, with ~91% showing Braak II–VI (i.e., will progress).
Special consideration for ADAD and DSAD: Florid striatal amyloid PET uptake often occurs early in ADAD/DSAD (not typical of sporadic AD). The Centiloid method and A+/A− thresholds may need modification in these populations. The indeterminant zone (confidently normal / indeterminant / confidently abnormal) should be applied for values near cutpoints.
Plasma Biomarker (T1) IF ACCURATE ASSAY AVAILABLE
Plasma p-tau217 (preferred) — or p-tau181, p-tau231 — using accurate validated assay
Plasma p-tau217 typically outperforms other plasma assays in head-to-head comparisons and in some cohorts achieves diagnostic accuracy equivalent to approved CSF assays (i.e., ≥90% accuracy for detecting amyloid PET positivity). The %p-tau217 ratio (p-tau217/non-phosphorylated-tau217) has high performance. Plasma Aβ42/40 has limited diagnostic range (~10–15% fold difference vs ~50% for CSF Aβ42/40) — generally less clinically robust than plasma p-tau assays.
Confounders of plasma biomarkers: Head trauma and cardiorespiratory arrest may transiently increase p-tau values. Elevated p-tau181 has been reported in ALS without AD. Certain medications (e.g., neprilysin inhibitors), CSF dynamics disorders, impaired renal function, and food intake can alter plasma biomarker values. Clinical judgment required.
CSF Hybrid Ratios FDA-APPROVED ASSAYS AVAILABLE
CSF Aβ42/40, CSF p-tau181/Aβ42, or CSF t-tau/Aβ42 — using approved or validated assay
CSF p-tau is not typically used as a standalone diagnostic test — hybrid ratios are used instead because they have the highest accuracy. Regulatory approval of CSF assays was anchored to amyloid PET positive/negative visual reads as reference standard (sensitivity/specificity 85–88% / 93–94%). CSF Aβ42/40 has ~50% fold difference between AD+ and AD− compared to only 10–15% for plasma Aβ42/40 — making CSF Aβ42/40 more diagnostically robust than its plasma counterpart.
Step 3 · Core 2 Biomarkers — Staging, Prognosis, and Symptom Attribution
Core 2 biomarkers become abnormal later — more closely linked to symptom onset. They cannot rule out AD (a normal Core 2 with abnormal Core 1 does not exclude AD), but an abnormal Core 2 in the presence of abnormal Core 1 increases confidence that AD is contributing to symptoms.
Core 2 biomarkers (T2 — AD tau proteinopathy):
Tau PET: The primary Core 2 imaging biomarker. Provides topographic and magnitude information. Stages: no uptake (A only) → MTL uptake → moderate neocortical → high neocortical uptake
CSF/plasma T2 fluid markers: MTBR-tau243, p-tau205, non-phosphorylated mid-region tau fragments (conceptual staging — less validated than tau PET)

Key principle: Amyloidosis is nearly always a prerequisite for neocortical AD tauopathy. A−T2+ profile is rare, inconsistent with AD, and corresponds neuropathologically to primary age-related tauopathy (PART) — not AD.
Tau PET (T2 — Core 2 Imaging) PRIMARY STAGING BIOMARKER
Tau PET result and topographic pattern
The neocortical meta-ROI includes inferior/lateral temporal, inferior/medial parietal lobes. The medial temporal ROI includes hippocampus (some ligands), entorhinal cortex, amygdala. PET-detectable medial temporal tauopathy does not always precede neocortical tauopathy in atypical presentations (e.g., posterior cortical atrophy, logopenic variant PPA), though MTL→neocortical spread is by far the most common pattern. Off-target tau ligand binding must be excluded (e.g., basal ganglia uptake). CenTauR standardization scale in development (analogous to Centiloid for amyloid).
Prognostic hazard ratios (from large meta-analysis, Ossenkoppele et al.): vs. A−T2− reference — A+T2− (Stage A): HR 1.5 for progression to dementia; A+ with MTL tau only (Stage B): HR 5.6; A+ with neocortical tau (Stages C/D): HR 39.9. A 40-fold range in hazard ratios across PET stages.
T2 Fluid Biomarkers (Conceptual — Less Validated) RESEARCH USE
CSF/plasma MTBR-tau243, p-tau205, or non-phosphorylated mid-region tau fragments
These later-changing tau fragments correlate more strongly with tau PET than with amyloid PET — distinguishing them from T1 markers (p-tau217, 181, 231 which correlate with both). The fluid staging scheme (Table 5 in the paper) mirrors Stages A–D but is regarded as conceptual at present: T1 fluid → p-tau205 (Stage B approximate) → MTBR-tau243 (Stage C approximate) → non-phosphorylated tau fragments (Stage D approximate). PET and fluid stages should not be treated as equivalent.
Step 4 · Biological Disease Staging (Stages A–D)
Biological staging applies only after AD is diagnosed via Core 1 biomarkers. It uses Core biomarkers only (N/I markers not included as staging criteria). Select the current biological stage.
Staging sequence (Table 3 — Jack et al. 2024):
Stage A (Initial): Abnormal amyloid PET (or Core 1 fluid) + no tau PET uptake. A+T2−
Stage B (Early): Abnormal amyloid PET + tau PET in medial temporal region only. A+T2MTL+
Stage C (Intermediate): Abnormal amyloid PET + moderate neocortical tau PET uptake. A+T2MOD+
Stage D (Advanced): Abnormal amyloid PET + high neocortical tau PET uptake. A+T2HIGH+

Core 1 fluid biomarkers establish Stage A or higher but cannot discriminate Stages A–D without tau PET.

Core 1 fluid only available (no tau PET) — Stage A or higher
If only Core 1 fluid biomarkers (plasma p-tau217 or CSF hybrid ratios) are available without tau PET, the individual can be confirmed as Stage A or higher, but stages B–D cannot be discriminated. If tau PET is subsequently available, it can be used to discriminate the stage. The conceptual fluid-only staging scheme (Table 5) using p-tau205, MTBR-tau243, and non-phosphorylated tau is available for research but not routinely validated for clinical staging.
Step 5 · Clinical Staging (Stages 0–6)
Clinical staging applies only to individuals in the AD pathophysiologic continuum (AD diagnosed by Core 1). It is independent of biological stage — the two axes are then integrated in the verdict. Select the current clinical stage.
Clinical stages (Table 6): Based on severity of cognitive/functional impairment — NOT phenotype. Any cognitive domain may be affected, not only memory. Five phenotypes recognized: amnestic/typical, language, visuospatial, behavioral, dysexecutive.

Stage 0: ADAD or DSAD — biomarker negative, asymptomatic (genetically determined from birth)
Stage 1: Asymptomatic — abnormal biomarker only, no clinical change
Stage 2: Transitional — mild detectable change, minimal functional impact, still fully independent
Stage 3: Cognitive impairment — objective impairment, early functional impact, still independent (≈MCI)
Stage 4: Dementia — mild functional impairment on instrumental ADLs, independent in basic ADLs (≈mild dementia)
Stage 5: Dementia — moderate functional impairment on basic ADLs requiring assistance (≈moderate dementia)
Stage 6: Dementia — severe impairment, complete dependence for basic ADLs (≈severe dementia)
ⓘ Stage 2 details: Decline from baseline within past 1–3 years, persistent ≥6 months. May be documented by: subtle longitudinal cognitive decline (within normal range), subjective report, or recent-onset mood/anxiety/motivational change not explained by life events. Individuals must have cognitive impairment (not just neurobehavioral) to reach Stages 3–6.
ⓘ APOE ε4: Not included in the staging scheme — regarded as a risk factor, not a stage. However, APOE ε4 homozygosity substantially increases ARIA risk with anti-Aβ immunotherapy; APOE screening recommended per FDA labeling for lecanemab.
Step 6 · NIVS Non-Core Biomarkers
N (neurodegeneration), I (inflammation), V (vascular), S (α-synuclein) biomarkers characterize the full neuropathophysiological state. They are important for prognosis, copathology identification, and clinical management — but are not part of the core diagnostic criteria.
N — Neurodegeneration / Neuronal Injury
NfL (neurofilament light chain) — plasma or CSF
NfL is a non-specific marker of large-caliber axonal injury. Elevated in MSA, ALS, MS, traumatic brain injury — not specific to AD. Useful as a non-specific prognostic marker of neurodegeneration severity. CSF total tau (removed from N category in 2024 compared to 2018) — its categorization remains unclear as it also increases in CJD, head trauma, anoxia, and infarction.
Anatomic MRI — hippocampal/cortical atrophy; FDG-PET — hypometabolism
I — Inflammation / Immune Activation
GFAP (glial fibrillary acidic protein) — astrocytic activation — plasma or CSF
GFAP reflects astrocytic reactivity. Not specific to AD but associated with early amyloid PET positivity, higher risk of incident dementia, and faster cognitive decline. Performs better in plasma than CSF for unclear reasons. Associated with progression even in cognitively normal individuals. Soluble TREM2 (CSF) reflects microglial reactivity — under investigation.
V — Vascular Brain Injury
MRI/CT vascular markers — infarctions (cortical, lacunar), white matter hyperintensities, CAA features (microbleeds, superficial siderosis)
Macroscopic infarctions and WMH are definitive biomarkers of ischemic vascular brain injury. CAA (cerebral amyloid angiopathy) is due to Aβ accumulation in vessel walls — distinct from traditional vascular risk factors but closely associated with Aβ plaques in AD. CAA on MRI (lobar microbleeds, superficial siderosis) increases ARIA risk with anti-Aβ immunotherapy — critical for treatment eligibility assessment. STRIVE-2 guidelines provide current neuroimaging standards for small vessel disease.
S — α-Synuclein Copathology
αSyn-SAA (α-synuclein seed amplification assay) — CSF only (plasma not informative)
αSyn-SAA is sensitive and specific for ante mortem identification of cortical α-synuclein pathology as a primary pathology or copathology. Cortical α-synuclein is associated with cognitive decline independent of amyloid and tau in both impaired and unimpaired individuals. Less sensitive to α-synuclein inclusions in MSA (different cellular location and conformation). No α-synuclein PET ligands are currently available for clinical use. DAT-SPECT/PET assesses striatal dopaminergic loss (relevant for suspected neuronal synuclein disease / Lewy body copathology).
